How a sentence states what it states: what the sources say (version of 11 November 2026).

1. One sentence, several propositions. "A hypodense lesion in the right kidney (cyst?)" states two: there is a
   lesion (stated) and it is a cyst (questioned). Each proposition has its own polarity, its own commitment of
   the writer, its own time and its own experiencer. Read each apart. (FactBank, Sauri and Pustejovsky: the
   factuality of an event is the commitment of a source to it: certain, probable or possible, positive or
   negative.)

2. Hedges are of two kinds (Prince, Frader and Bosk, "On hedging in physician-physician discourse", 1982).
   - Approximators change the content of the proposition: adaptors bear on class membership ("sort of",
     "somewhat", "-like", "a little"), rounders give a range ("about", "approximately", "around"). "A faint
     ground-glass-like density" is a density of that look: stated, not doubted.
   - Shields leave the content as it is and say how far the speaker commits to its truth: plausibility shields
     express doubt ("I think", "probably", "as far as I can tell"); attribution shields name the source of the
     knowledge ("according to", "presumably"). A source is not a doubt: "when evaluated together with US, it
     was understood that it belonged to biliary sludge" states the sludge, known from the ultrasound.
   - What a word does in its sentence decides, not the word (Skelton 1988: an approximator can work as a shield).
   Hyland (1996) calls the first attribute hedges ("generally", "approximately") and the second reliability
   hedges ("possible", "may", "probably"). Only the reliability, the plausibility shield, is an uncertainty.

3. Speculation and negation (BioScope, Vincze et al. 2008, which includes radiology reports).
   - A sentence is speculative when "neither its existence nor its non-existence is unequivocally stated".
     Cues: "suggest", "may", "indicate", "or" between alternatives, a question.
   - "Cannot be excluded" is a speculation, not a negation.
   - Negation is "the implication of the non-existence of something": "no", "without", "absent". A negative
     word does not by itself make the sentence negative.
   - The cue is the smallest unit that expresses it; its scope is the largest unit it bears on. Read what the
     cue bears on: a question mark bears on what it follows or closes, a parenthesis with "?" on its words.

4. Time and experiencer (ConText, Harkema et al. 2009). A condition is recent, historical (of the past, "in
   the previous CT", "known", "history of") or hypothetical ("if", "should it"); it is of the patient or of
   someone else (a relative). "Stable", "unchanged", "new", "decreased", "regressed" state a change in time:
   certain.

5. The commitment has levels (radiology).
   - Panicek and Hricak, AJR 2016: "consistent with" more than 90%; "suspicious for", "probably" about 75%;
     "possibly" about 50%; "less likely" about 25%; "unlikely" less than 10%.
   - The Diagnostic Certainty Scale of Brigham (Shinagare et al.; Hammer et al., JACR 2020): "consistent with"
     a known diagnosis; "most likely" more than 90%; "likely" 75 to 90%; "may represent" 25 to 75%; "unlikely"
     5 to 25%; "very unlikely" less than 5%.
   - The same phrase is read differently by different readers (Hobby et al., Br J Radiol 2000); radiologists
     are overconfident with "very likely" and underconfident with "possibly" (Wang et al., ICLR 2025). Read the
     level from the whole sentence: "may be consistent with" is not "consistent with"; "a suspicious density
     consistent with a calculus" is suspicious, not consistent.

6. What is neither stated nor doubted. "Nonspecific", "uncharacterized", "cannot be characterized": what is
   seen is stated; only its nature is open (no proposition of what it is is made, so none is doubted). "As far
   as can be observed", "within the limits of non-contrast CT": the exam's limit; it bears on what could not be
   seen, most often an absence, never on what was seen.

7. One place for each word. A word that is an approximation, a source, a time, an open nature or a limit is
   not also an uncertainty. When the writer commits, the proposition is stated; when the writer shields it, it
   is uncertain, at the level the sentence gives.
